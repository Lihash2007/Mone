PERSONAL MONEY OS
Product Requirements & Technical Blueprint
India-first personal finance platform for iOS and Android
Master Product Document • Version 1.0 • October 2026
1. Executive Summary
Personal Money OS is an India-first cross-platform personal finance application designed to give users one clear view of their money across UPI, bank accounts, cards, cash, recurring payments, peer-to-peer transfers, savings goals and investments. The product is intended to solve a practical problem: digital payments make spending effortless, but financial visibility remains fragmented.
The app will combine transaction intelligence, budgeting, cash-flow planning, financial goals and an AI assistant. The central experience is not simply showing historical expenses; it is helping users understand where money went, what commitments are coming next, and what amount is available for discretionary spending.
The product will be built for both iOS and Android, with a shared mobile codebase, a secure backend, a transaction-normalization layer, and an independent AI/ML layer. India-specific integrations and regulatory requirements will be treated as architecture-level concerns from the beginning.
2. Product Vision
Vision: Build a personal financial operating system for everyday users in India.
Core promise:
Know where your money is going, what is coming next, and how much you can safely spend.
2.1 Product Principles
India-first: UPI, cash, bank transfers, cards and Indian spending patterns are first-class concepts.
Simple by default: users should understand their financial position without reading accounting terminology.
Explainable intelligence: automated classifications and insights should show enough context for users to correct them.
User control: users decide categories, budgets, goals and whether an automated interpretation is correct.
Privacy and security by design: financial information is sensitive and must be minimized, protected and auditable.
Modular architecture: payment-data integrations, AI models and financial features must be replaceable without rebuilding the mobile client.

7.1 UPI Peer-to-Peer Classification
A payment to a person cannot reliably be treated as an expense from the transaction alone. The app should present a lightweight confirmation when confidence is low, such as: Food split, Loan, Gift, Rent, Transfer, or Other. User corrections should improve future classification.

8.1 Explainability Requirement
AI outputs should distinguish observed transaction facts from inference. For example, the app may say 'This merchant was categorized as Food based on previous transactions' rather than presenting a classification as certain when the evidence is weak.

13. Security, Privacy and Compliance
Use encryption in transit and at rest.
Never store UPI PINs, OTPs, CVVs, bank passwords or other authentication secrets.
Use least-privilege access for services and data.
Separate authentication secrets from financial records.
Maintain audit logs for sensitive account and data-access events.
Provide consent records and data-access transparency.
Provide account/data export and deletion workflows where applicable.
Minimize personally identifiable and financial data collected.
Use secure mobile storage for tokens and device credentials.
Perform threat modeling, penetration testing and dependency/security scanning before production launch.
Treat RBI/NPCI, privacy and other applicable Indian regulatory requirements as a dedicated compliance workstream; validate the exact obligations for the final business model and integrations with qualified legal/compliance professionals before launch.
13.1 Account Aggregator Strategy
The architecture should be designed so consent-based financial data connectivity can be integrated without coupling the mobile app to a particular provider. The product should evaluate India's Account Aggregator ecosystem and applicable regulated-partner requirements before implementing live financial-data connectivity. The initial prototype can use manual/imported transaction data while the integration and compliance model is validated.

16. MVP Acceptance Criteria
A user can create an account securely on iOS and Android.
A user can add bank/card/cash accounts manually.
A user can record and edit income and expenses.
A transaction can be categorized and corrected.
Transfers do not inflate expense totals.
Credit-card payments do not duplicate the original card purchase.
Users can create budgets and see live progress.
Users can create savings goals and track progress.
Recurring payments can be identified and displayed.
The dashboard provides a coherent monthly financial summary.
The application remains useful even without automated bank connectivity.
Sensitive credentials such as UPI PINs and OTPs are never collected or stored.

19. Key Product Differentiator
The product should not compete merely as another expense tracker. Its differentiation is the combination of India-specific transaction intelligence, UPI-aware classification, cash/card/bank unification, forward-looking commitment tracking, safe-to-spend planning and an explainable AI financial assistant.

22. Final Product Definition
Personal Money OS is envisioned as a cross-platform personal financial management application for India. Its first job is to make everyday money understandable. Its second job is to make future commitments visible. Its third job is to reduce the manual effort required to maintain a financial plan.
The application should therefore be built around a reliable transaction ledger and a strong data model first, with AI layered on top of trustworthy financial records. Automation, UPI-aware intelligence and future financial connectivity should extend that foundation rather than replace it.
